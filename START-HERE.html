Image SEO Engine

Upload a picture. It writes the SEO and publishes it for you.

What you get: every picture gets its own web page with a title, a description, the hidden tags Google reads, and an optional Buy button that goes to your shop. It also makes a feed that Pinterest can turn into pins automatically. Hosting is free on GitHub.
Before you start: setup takes about 20 minutes, once, and you need a computer for steps 1 to 6. After that, you can add pictures from your phone.

1Make a free GitHub account

Go to github.com/signup and sign up. Your username becomes part of your website address, so pick something simple.

2Make a new repository (your site's folder)

  1. On GitHub, tap the + at the top right and choose New repository.
  2. Name it something like my-gallery.
  3. Choose Public. Free websites need a public repository.
  4. Check Add a README file, then click Create repository.

3Upload the files from this kit

  1. Unzip the kit on your computer.
  2. In your new repository, click Add file, then Upload files.
  3. Open the unzipped folder, select everything inside it (all files and folders), and drag it into the upload box.
  4. Click Commit changes.

4Add the robot (one file)

Your computer may hide the .github folder, so it might not have uploaded. Check: if you see a .github folder in your repository, skip this step. If not:

  1. Click Add file, then Create new file.
  2. For the name, type exactly: .github/workflows/build.yml
  3. Open robot-workflow.txt from the kit, copy all of it, and paste it into the big box.
  4. Click Commit changes.

5Put in your info

In your repository, click config.json, then the pencil icon to edit. Change the words between the quotes:

SettingWhat to put
site_titleYour gallery's name, e.g. "Rosa's Pottery"
author_nameYour name or business name
site_descriptionOne sentence about what you make or sell
shop_urlWhere the Buy button goes for every picture, e.g. your shop or website. Leave "" for no button.
shop_button_textThe button's words, e.g. "Shop now"
base_urlLeave it "". It fills in your free address by itself. Only change it if you connect your own domain (step 10).

Keep the quotes and commas exactly as they are. Click Commit changes.

6Turn on your website

  1. In your repository, click Settings, then Pages on the left.
  2. Under Branch, choose main, then choose the folder /docs, and click Save.
  3. Your site's address is https://YOUR-USERNAME.github.io/my-gallery/ (use your username and repository name). It can take a few minutes to appear the first time.

7Add pictures (works on your phone)

  1. Open the images folder in your repository.
  2. Click Add file, then Upload files, and pick your pictures (JPG, PNG or WEBP).
  3. Click Commit changes. In about 2 minutes, each picture has its own page on your site.
Tip: name your pictures with words, like blue-ceramic-vase.jpg, not IMG_4021.jpg. The name becomes the page title and web address. Or turn on the AI writer (step 9) and it writes them for you.

Give one picture its own Buy link, price and description (optional)

Upload a text file with the same name as the picture, ending in .txt. For blue-ceramic-vase.jpg, make blue-ceramic-vase.txt:

Shop: https://your-shop.com/blue-vase
Price: $24
Button: Buy this vase

A glossy blue stoneware vase, handmade in small batches.

Every line is optional. A link must start with https://. To make one on GitHub: in the images folder, click Add file, then Create new file, and name it.

8Check it worked

Click the Actions tab in your repository. A green check means your site was updated. A red X means something went wrong. Click it to see the message (usually a typo in config.json, like a missing quote or comma).

9Turn on the free AI writer (optional, recommended)

This makes a free AI look at each new picture and write its title and description.

  1. Go to aistudio.google.com/apikey, sign in with Google, and click Create API key. Don't turn on billing. The free plan is enough.
  2. Copy the key.
  3. In your repository: Settings, then Secrets and variables, then Actions, then New repository secret.
  4. Name: GEMINI_API_KEY. Secret: paste your key. Click Add secret.

The robot's drafts are saved as name.ai.txt in the images folder. To change its words, edit that file, or write your own name.txt, which always wins. Note: pictures are sent to Google to be described, and on the free plan Google may use them to improve its products.

10Get found on Google and Pinterest

Google: sign in to Google Search Console, add your site address, and choose the HTML tag way to verify. Copy only the code inside content="..." into google_verify in config.json, wait 2 minutes, and click Verify. Then go to Sitemaps and submit sitemap.xml.

Pinterest (automatic pins): Pinterest only auto-pins from a website you have claimed, and claiming works best with your own domain name (like yourshop.com, usually about $10 to $15 a year).

  1. Connect your domain: in Settings → Pages, enter it under Custom domain and follow GitHub's instructions. Then set base_url in config.json to it, e.g. "https://gallery.yourshop.com".
  2. Make a free Pinterest business account. In its settings, claim your website with the HTML tag method, and copy the code into pinterest_verify in config.json.
  3. In Pinterest settings, under Auto-publish, paste https://your-site-address/rss.xml, pick a board, and save. New pictures become pins within about a day.

Without a claimed website, everything else still works. You can pin your pages to Pinterest by hand.

Questions

  • Does it cost anything? No. GitHub hosting and the AI writer are free at normal sizes. Only a custom domain costs money, and it's optional.
  • How do I remove a picture? Delete it (and its .txt files) from the images folder. Its page disappears on the next update.
  • Can I change the colors? Yes. The page design is in templates/image-page.html.
  • Phone upload page: the worker folder has an advanced add-on for uploading without GitHub. It's optional and needs a free Cloudflare account. See worker/README.md.
